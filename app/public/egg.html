Physics C: Electricity and Magnetism · Free-Response Practice

2 questions · Suggested time: 30 minutes · Show all your work

Question 1 15 points

A nonconducting solid sphere of radius \(R\) has a nonuniform volume charge density \(\rho(r) = \rho_0\left(1 - \dfrac{r}{R}\right)\) for \(r \le R\), where \(\rho_0\) is a positive constant and \(r\) is the distance from the center. The sphere is surrounded by a concentric, uncharged conducting spherical shell of inner radius \(2R\) and outer radius \(3R\), as shown in Figure 1. Express all algebraic answers in terms of \(\rho_0\), \(R\), \(r\), and fundamental constants, as appropriate.

  1. Derive an expression for the total charge \(Q\) on the nonconducting sphere.
  2. Using Gauss's law, derive an expression for the magnitude of the electric field \(E(r)\) in each of the following regions.
    1. \(r < R\)
    2. \(R < r < 2R\)
    3. \(2R < r < 3R\)
    4. \(r > 3R\)
  3. Determine the distance \(r\) from the center at which the electric field inside the nonconducting sphere is a maximum. Justify your answer.
  4. On the axes below, sketch a graph of \(E\) as a function of \(r\) for \(0 \le r \le 4R\).
  5. Derive an expression for the potential difference \(V(R) - V(2R)\) between the surface of the nonconducting sphere and the inner surface of the shell.
  6. Determine the surface charge density \(\sigma\) on the inner surface of the conducting shell.
  7. The outer surface of the shell is now connected to Earth by a conducting wire. Indicate whether the magnitude of the electric field at \(r = \tfrac{3}{2}R\) increases, decreases, or remains the same. Justify your answer.

Question 2 15 points

Two long, parallel, frictionless conducting rails of negligible resistance are separated by a distance \(\ell\) and lie in a plane inclined at angle \(\theta\) above the horizontal. The rails are connected at the top by an initially uncharged capacitor of capacitance \(C\). A uniform magnetic field of magnitude \(B\) is directed perpendicular to the plane of the rails, as shown in Figure 2. A conducting bar of mass \(m\) and negligible resistance is released from rest on the rails at time \(t = 0\) and remains perpendicular to them as it slides. Express all algebraic answers in terms of \(m\), \(\ell\), \(\theta\), \(C\), \(B\), \(t\), and physical constants, as appropriate.

  1. On a dot representing the bar, draw and label the forces (not components) exerted on the bar while it is sliding down the rails.
  2. Write an expression for the current \(I\) in the bar in terms of the bar's acceleration \(a\).
  3. Derive an expression for the acceleration \(a\) of the bar. Show that the bar moves with constant acceleration.
  4. Derive an expression for the energy stored in the capacitor as a function of time \(t\).
  5. Show that the total mechanical energy lost by the bar at time \(t\) equals the energy stored in the capacitor. Explain any discrepancy, or explain why there is none.
  6. The capacitor is replaced by a resistor of resistance \(R_0\), and the bar is again released from rest.
    1. Write, but do not solve, a differential equation for the speed \(v\) of the bar.
    2. Solve the differential equation to determine \(v(t)\).
    3. Derive an expression for the terminal speed \(v_T\) of the bar.
